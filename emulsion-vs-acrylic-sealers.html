Asphalt Emulsion vs Acrylic Sealers and Runoff Restrictions

Homeowners choosing between DIY buckets and contractor spray rigs encounter two main modern sealer families—asphalt emulsion and acrylic—alongside legacy coal-tar products that many US cities and states now restrict or ban.

  • Asphalt emulsion sealers: Water-based bitumen emulsions with lower odor and minimal polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) levels compared with coal tar; widely used for residential driveways when mixed with fine silica sand for traction.
  • Acrylic sealers: Polymer-based coatings that dry quickly, resist UV fading, and hold color well, though material cost is higher and surface cleanliness requirements are strict.
  • Local coal-tar regulations: USGS water-resources research has documented elevated PAH runoff from coal-tar-based sealcoats into urban streams and sediments, prompting numerous municipal and state bans. Always check local ordinances and ask contractors for the product safety data sheet (SDS) in advance.
