Asphalt Driveway Sealing Prep Guide: Inspect, Clean and Patch First

Sealcoating protects sound asphalt pavement from ultraviolet oxidation, water infiltration, and minor oil drips—but sealer is a thin wear coating, not a structural fix. Applying sealer over loose gravel, unsealed cracks, or a failing sub-base traps moisture and peels within one winter.

The Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) notes that asphalt pavements rely on both the surface binder and a drained, compacted aggregate base underneath. Because driveways also shed rainwater directly toward street storm drains, the U.S. EPA's stormwater guidance emphasizes preventing wash-off and pollutants from entering local waterways during exterior maintenance.

Pre-sealing driveway walk-around

Sealer cost data, prep walkthroughs, and repair comparisons are published on Driveway Sealing Lab.

  1. Wait on brand-new asphalt: Freshly paved asphalt needs months of warm-weather oxidation so light oils evaporate; sealing too soon softens the surface and causes tire scuffing.
  2. Clear weeds and dirt out of every crack: Wire-brush and blow cracks dry before filling; sealer bridges nothing wider than a hairline.
  3. Spot-treat oil stains: Fresh motor-oil spots dissolve asphalt binder and reject water-based sealers unless degreased and primed first.
  4. Check edge drainage: Look for standing puddles or downspout water dumping onto pavement edges—fixing runoff stops freeze-thaw heaving.
Do not over-seal every year. Thick stacked layers of unnecessary sealcoat become brittle and craze into fine surface cracks faster than a driveway sealed only every two to four years as surface aggregate begins to show.
